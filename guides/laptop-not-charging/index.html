Laptop Not Charging? 7 Quick Fixes

Quick answer: Most "laptop not charging" problems come from a loose cable, a faulty charger or a stuck power state. Try another wall socket, then unplug everything and hold the power button for 30 seconds before plugging back in.

If your laptop says "plugged in, not charging" or the charging light stays off, work through the fixes below in order. Test after each step so you know what solved it.

1. Check the socket and cable

Plug the charger into a different wall socket and check that the cable is fully pushed into both the wall adapter and the laptop. Look along the cable for kinks, cuts or bent pins. Many chargers fail where the cable meets the plug.

2. Look at the charger light and the charging port

Most chargers have a small LED. If it is off, the charger is probably dead. Check the laptop's charging port for dust or lint with a flashlight, and gently clear it with a wooden toothpick while the laptop is off and unplugged. A loose port that wobbles needs a technician.

3. Do a power reset

A power reset clears stuck power states:

  1. Shut down the laptop and unplug the charger.
  2. If the battery is removable, take it out.
  3. Hold the power button for 30 seconds.
  4. Put the battery back, plug in the charger and turn the laptop on.

4. Reinstall the battery driver (Windows)

  1. Right-click Start and open Device Manager.
  2. Expand Batteries.
  3. Right-click Microsoft ACPI-Compliant Control Method Battery and choose Uninstall device.
  4. Restart the laptop. Windows reinstalls the driver automatically.

5. Try a different compatible charger

If you can borrow a charger with the same voltage and connector, test with it. If the laptop charges, your original charger is faulty. Always match the voltage and wattage printed on your original adapter.

6. Check your battery health

Open Command Prompt and run powercfg /batteryreport. Open the HTML file it creates and compare Design capacity with Full charge capacity. If the full charge capacity is far lower, the battery is worn out and needs replacing.

7. Update Windows and the BIOS

Install all Windows updates, then check your laptop maker's support page for a BIOS or firmware update. Charging bugs are sometimes fixed there.

Tip: Some laptops pause charging at 80% on purpose to protect the battery. Check your manufacturer's battery app before assuming something is broken.

When to see a technician

If the charger works on another laptop, the battery report looks healthy and none of the steps above help, the charging port or motherboard may be damaged. Stop using damaged chargers, and take the laptop to a repair shop.
